# Bogle Core Philosophy

Use this reference for the governing investment philosophy. It synthesizes concepts from John C. Bogle's broader body of work in original language rather than treating any one book as the entire framework.

## Source map

| Work | Decision role in this skill |
|---|---|
| *Enough* | Define the purpose and sufficiency of wealth before optimizing returns |
| *Clash of the Cultures* | Distinguish investment from speculation and examine financial-industry incentives |
| *Common Sense on Mutual Funds* | Evaluate fund economics, diversification, cost, tax drag, selection, and persistence |
| *The Little Book of Common Sense Investing* | Provide a concise entry to the indexing and cost-matters case |
| *Stay the Course* | Preserve long-horizon discipline through market and narrative changes |
| *John Bogle on Investing* | Connect market returns, costs, governance, and the investor's share of returns |
| *Don't Count On It!* | Apply skepticism to forecasts, promises, historical performance, and false precision |

The works are intellectual inputs, not scripts to quote. Apply their principles to the user's actual evidence and constraints.

## Bogle principle cards

All `BG` cards have `default` authority inside long-term investment decisions.

| ID | Principle | Scope | Operational rule | Boundary | Prohibited inference | Source roots |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `BG-01` | Purpose and Enough precede portfolio optimization | Goals and investment purpose | Establish what money is for, the time horizon, and what loss would damage the life plan | Enough is personal | A universal wealth target can be inferred from Bogle | `BGL-ENOUGH`, `BGL-STC` |
| `BG-02` | Own productive economic activity | Asset role | Treat a portfolio as claims on businesses and contractual cash flows | Not every quoted asset is productive | A quoted price proves an investment has an economic return source | `BGL-COC`, `BGL-JBOI` |
| `BG-03` | Capture the market return efficiently | Long-term implementation | Begin from broad diversification rather than assumed selection skill | Legal, tax, access, or liquidity constraints may change implementation | Broad indexing eliminates loss | `BGL-CSMF`, `BGL-LB` |
| `BG-04` | Costs compound negatively | Products, trading, advice, taxes | Count product, trading, tax, advice, time, and behavioral costs | Lowest headline fee is not always lowest total cost | A low-cost product is automatically suitable | `BGL-CSMF`, `BGL-JBOI` |
| `BG-05` | Investor behavior can destroy investment return | Risk and implementation | Test whether the user can maintain the policy through severe volatility | Willingness does not replace financial capacity | A questionnaire proves future behavior | `BGL-CSMF`, `BGL-STC` |
| `BG-06` | Forecasts are weak policy foundations | Forecasts and performance records | Use ranges and historical evidence, not rankings or precise predictions | Forecasts may support scenarios | A precise forecast deserves policy authority | `BGL-DCI`, `BGL-CSMF` |
| `BG-07` | Investment and speculation require different labels | Active, leveraged, or narrative assets | Label price-dependent or short-horizon activity honestly | Intended holding period alone does not decide the label | Calling an asset long term makes it an investment | `BGL-COC` |
| `BG-08` | Simplicity reduces failure modes | Product and rule design | Add products or rules only for a material, understandable benefit | Simplicity cannot ignore a material risk | More funds necessarily create more diversification | `BGL-LB`, `BGL-STC` |
| `BG-09` | Stay the course under a sound policy | Policy continuity | Ignore ordinary price volatility; review when personal premises change | Do not preserve a broken policy | Staying the course means never reviewing the plan | `BGL-STC`, `BGL-DCI` |
| `BG-10` | Stewardship and fiduciary structure matter | Products, managers, institutions | Examine whose interests the structure serves | Good intentions do not replace incentives and results | Brand reputation proves fiduciary alignment | `BGL-BSC`, `BGL-CHAR`, `BGL-ARCH` |

## Default decision test

Before recommending a portfolio change, ask:

- What happens if the user continues the existing sound, low-cost, diversified policy?
- Is the proposal based on enduring needs or recent market experience?
- What total cost and tax drag will it introduce?
- Does it improve diversification, risk alignment, or implementability?
- Can the user understand and maintain it through a severe drawdown?
- Is any part of the proposal actually speculation?

If the proposed improvement is unclear after costs, taxes, complexity, and behavioral risk, prefer the simpler policy or no change.
