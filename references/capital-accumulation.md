# Wage-Led Capital Accumulation

Use this reference when employment income and human capital are the user's primary wealth engine. Protect earning capacity and resilience, convert sustainable surplus into long-term productive assets, and track the gradual transition toward financial-capital participation without inventing return attribution.

## Stage profile

Establish the current wealth stage, primary wealth engine, accumulation mechanism, and desired life transition. Treat this as current context, not a permanent identity.

## Accumulation loop

1. Protect health, employability, and the income engine.
2. Preserve legal obligations, solvency, and required liquidity.
3. Create repeatable surplus rather than a one-off high saving month.
4. Execute the passive accumulation policy consistently.
5. Limit fees, taxes, speculation, and complexity that interrupt compounding.
6. Review whether dependence on salary is falling without weakening present life.

Do not praise a high contribution amount when it damages an earlier boundary.

## Evidence available in v0.7

From `03 Monthly History`:

- earned income = salary + other earned income;
- earned-income-funded surplus = earned income - living expense;
- net saving = earned income - living expense + other external flow;
- saving rate, with its denominator stated;
- trends in native-currency assets when comparable months have frozen CNY, USD, and HKD totals.

From `04 Investment Flow`:

- actual investment, FX, dividend, interest, and fee events by date;
- actual monthly passive-buy amount for the asset and currency named in `01 Rules`;
- execution variance from that user-defined monthly target.

From `02 Balance Sheet`:

- current positions and local-currency values as of the snapshot date;
- liquidity facts supported by named cash and reserve assets.

The workbook cannot, by itself, convert all currencies into one net worth, split asset growth into contribution versus market return, or prove that a particular salary unit funded a particular purchase. Never create a capital bridge or market/FX plug from these data.

## Policy-to-execution bridge

Use this sequence instead of a net-worth attribution bridge:

```text
Economic-month earned income
→ living expense and external flows
→ net saving in CNY
→ actual FX and investment events on their dates
→ native-currency asset positions at the next valid snapshot
```

The arrows express a process, not one-to-one tracing. A CNY saving total, an FX Buy, and a passive purchase can occur at different dates and amounts. Report each fact independently.

Read the policy asset, currency, and target from the named monthly contribution rule in `01 Rules`, then compare matching `Passive Buy` events:

```text
variance = actual qualifying passive buys - configured monthly target
```

Classify the month as `Met`, `Below`, `Above`, or `No qualifying event`, but do not label a variance good or bad before checking liquidity, fees, execution timing, and the user's explanation. Never backfill a missing event or change its date to force adherence.

## Transition indicators

Use trends rather than universal targets:

| Indicator | Meaning | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Net-saving continuity | Whether employment income repeatedly creates surplus | One month is not a trend |
| Resilience after saving | Whether cash floors and obligations remain protected | High investment can still be fragile |
| Passive-policy execution | Matching passive buys versus the written monthly rule | Target adherence is not investment performance |
| Native-currency asset growth | Change in CNY, USD, or HKD assets separately | Includes flows and valuation changes |
| Distribution trend | Dividends and interest recorded in Investment Flow | Distributions are not total return |
| Income-engine resilience | Stability, replacement time, and capability investment | The workbook does not establish employability |

Do not declare financial independence, a stage transition, or capital-funded growth share from these indicators alone.

## Decision priority

Repair legal, solvency, liquidity, or catastrophic-risk gaps first; then protect earning capacity; then continue the simple passive rule; then fund chosen current-life priorities consistent with Enough; consider bounded active capital last. Active capital is ineligible when its permanent loss would interrupt passive accumulation or require future labor income to repair.

## Output

Report the current stage, income-engine and liquidity constraints, economic-month saving, actual passive-policy execution, native-currency asset trends, data limits, and the smallest next action. Do not turn the review into product hunting or portfolio optimization.
