# Consumption and Enough

Use this reference when judging spending, lifestyle inflation, frugality, major purchases, or whether investing is crowding out present life. Apply `FC-01`, `FC-02`, and `BG-01` before category-level optimization.

## Governing idea

Spending is not automatically the enemy of investing. Judge whether spending is affordable, intentional, aligned with the user's values, and compatible with financial resilience. Lower spending is not inherently better when it damages health, relationships, earning capacity, or a deliberately chosen life priority.

## Spending roles

| Role | Primary question |
|---|---|
| Essential living | Is it necessary, stable, and supportable? |
| Risk protection | Does it reduce a material downside, or is it unnecessary duplication? |
| Capability building | Does it improve health, language, professional skill, or earning durability? |
| Life quality | Does it create recurring value consistent with the user's Enough? |
| Convenience | Is the time or cognitive load saved worth the recurring cost? |
| Status or impulse | Would the user still choose it without comparison, urgency, or social signaling? |
| One-time major purchase | Was it planned, is the full cost understood, and does it impair liquidity or a higher priority? |

## Decision questions

1. What value or problem does the spending address?
2. Is it recurring, irregular, or one-time?
3. Is it affordable after obligations and required liquidity, not merely affordable from this month's income?
4. What higher-priority goal or freedom does it displace?
5. Does it strengthen or weaken future earning capacity and health?
6. Would the decision still feel right without social comparison or short-term emotion?
7. What pattern appears over several months rather than one unusual month?

## Diagnostic boundaries

- Do not use a universal consumption percentage as a moral standard.
- Do not call a month unhealthy solely because a planned annual expense occurred.
- Do not classify an investment purchase as consumption.
- Do not praise extreme saving when it creates fragile liquidity or an unsustainable life.
- Do not excuse repeated low-value spending merely because the savings rate remains high.

## Recommended output

State whether the pattern is `Aligned`, `Watch`, `Misaligned`, or `Insufficient Data`; explain affordability, value alignment, opportunity cost, and sustainability separately. Recommend a rule or review trigger rather than guilt about individual small purchases.
