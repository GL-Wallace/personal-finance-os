# Active-Asset Type Router

Classify the proposed exception before analyzing it. Use the narrowest applicable path.

| Asset or decision | Governing analysis | Do not substitute |
|---|---|---|
| Broad, low-cost market index fund | Bogle philosophy, Bogleheads implementation, portfolio policy | Individual-company valuation |
| Individual listed company | Buffett–Munger principles plus active-investment workflow | Recent price action or simple multiple screens |
| Sector or thematic index fund | Industry structure, cycle, concentration, valuation, overlap, and timing burden | Treating diversification within one sector as broad diversification |
| Active mutual fund or external manager | Mandate, process, people, capacity, fees, turnover, tax drag, benchmark, and persistence | Underwriting only the current holdings |
| Bond or credit security | Contract terms, seniority, default probability, recovery, duration, liquidity, currency, and tax | Equity-style growth narratives |
| Property or private business | Cash yield, maintenance capital, financing, legal title, concentration, liquidity, and operating burden | Public-market liquidity assumptions |
| Commodity, cryptoasset, collectible, or pre-profit narrative asset without reliable owner earnings | Value anchor, custody, liquidity, regulatory risk, position sizing, and explicit speculation label | A discounted-cash-flow model without cash flows |
| Market timing, leverage, or derivatives | Edge, payoff distribution, path dependency, financing, counterparty risk, taxes, and maximum loss | Long-term-investment language |

## Mixed cases

If an asset spans categories, apply every material path and use the most conservative conclusion. For example, a concentrated technology ETF requires both fund-level structure analysis and sector-cycle analysis.

If the asset cannot be classified, stop at `Needs More Data`. Classification determines what evidence can answer the decision.
